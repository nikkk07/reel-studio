## Research plan (use WebSearch and WebFetch; open the pages you rely on)
1. **The business itself**: the website's home, about, services/courses/products, pricing or fees pages if published, FAQ, contact, blog. What it sells, to whom, where, since when, how it works, what makes it different. Use only what the business itself publishes or an independent source confirms; never invent numbers, awards, client counts or claims.
2. **Its social presence**: open the profile links if they load (Instagram/LinkedIn/Facebook often need a login; then use search results about them). Note its tone and what it already posts.
3. **The market**: the 3-6 most relevant competitors (what they promise, their content angle, where they are weak), the trends and seasons that drive demand now, and the questions customers actually ask (Google "people also ask", Reddit/Quora, YouTube titles).
4. **The rules**: advertising and content rules of this industry in its country (for example lawyers in India may not solicit or advertise under the Bar Council of India rules, so their reels must be general legal awareness, never an offer of services; aviation training cannot promise jobs or licences; finance/health have disclosure rules). Turn them into hard rules.
5. **Instagram strategy**: the content pillars, split into the two lanes:
   - `core`: the business's own expertise, services and the concepts its customers must understand (identity, trust, leads);
   - `trending`: what its audience talks about now (reach), still clearly right for this account.
   Write hook patterns that fit this audience, and a topic bank of at least 16 ready topics (at least 10 core), each with a scroll-stopping hook, the 1-3 facts it relies on and a real source URL.
6. **Look**: the visual style its reels should have (stock footage keywords, what to avoid, graphics), in line with its brand.
